FindMe · working name

A great product still needs
a place to buy it.

Everything on the first page is about what happens once a parent has a band in hand. Before that, someone has to find FindMe, trust it, and pay for it. That's a separate piece of work, and it's worth starting now so the store is ready when your first batch is.

Nothing on this page is a quote. It's a list of what has to exist before the first band sells, so we can talk through which parts you want to handle and where I can help.

01
This one comes first

Your web address

Every band carries a code that points to a web address. Once a batch is printed, that address is on those bands for as long as they exist. So the domain has to be chosen and bought before your first batch goes to the manufacturer, and it should be one you'll be happy with for years.

  • Short, easy to say out loud, and easy to type on a phone
  • Registered in FindMe's name, in an account you control, the same as everything else
  • One address carries your store, the page a helper sees, and your email
Why this can't wait

Almost everything else can be fixed later with an update. A band printed with the wrong address can't. It's the one decision that's permanent the moment the bands ship.

02
Where parents buy

An online store

You don't need a store built from scratch. My recommendation is Shopify. It handles the parts that are easy to get wrong: taking payment securely, calculating sales tax, printing shipping labels, and sending order confirmations. Parents already trust its checkout, which matters when you're a brand they've never heard of.

The store and the FindMe system stay separate at first. A parent buys a band, it arrives in the mail, and they scan it to set it up. That means the store doesn't change anything about Step 2, and it can go live on its own schedule.

03
What makes someone buy

Telling the story

A parent deciding whether to put this on their child wants to understand it in about thirty seconds. The store needs to show them:

  • Good photos of the band, on a real kid, in real places
  • How it works, in three simple steps
  • A clear answer to "what happens to my child's information?" Your privacy design is a selling point, so say so
  • Pricing that makes sense for families with more than one child
  • Answers to the questions people ask before they buy

The store will also need a privacy policy and terms of sale. Those belong in the attorney review from the first page, so it's one conversation instead of two.

04
Launch

Getting the first hundred out the door

A hundred bands is a small enough number to sell to people you can reach directly. Questions worth talking through together:

  • What does a band sell for? And is there a family pack?
  • Who buys the first hundred? Friends and family, schools, youth sports leagues, local events?
  • Shipped or handed over? Mailing every order is different work than selling at a ball field.
  • What happens when one breaks or gets lost? Your replacement and return policy.
  • What does a successful first batch look like? Knowing that number in advance makes the Step 3 decision easy.
05
Dividing it up

What's yours and where I can help

Some of this only the two of you can decide. A lot of the rest is setup work that's easy for someone who's done it before and a frustrating month for someone who hasn't.

Yours to decide
  • The name and the web address
  • What a band sells for
  • How the brand looks and sounds
  • Who your first buyers are
  • Photos of the product in use
Where I can help
  • Buying the domain and setting up email, in FindMe's name
  • Building and configuring the store
  • Writing the store's pages with you
  • Connecting the address to both the store and the scan page
Let's talk about this

None of this has to be decided on the day. It's here so the store is ready when the bands are, instead of a box of bands waiting for a place to sell them.
